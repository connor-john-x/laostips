Laos is generally cheaper than major Chinese cities, but inflation has been noticeable recently and costs vary a lot by city and lifestyle. Here is a breakdown for the capital, Vientiane.

## Housing

- **Shared room**: about USD 150–350 per month.
- **One-bedroom apartment**: about USD 300–600, more downtown or furnished.
- **Utilities**: electricity is metered and air-conditioning raises bills in hot season; internet is about USD 20–40 per month.

## Food

- **Local eateries**: about USD 1.5–3 per meal (Lao noodles, sticky rice).
- **Chinese / Western restaurants**: about USD 5–15 per meal.
- **Cooking at home**: local market produce is cheap; imported goods are pricey.

Eating out for all meals runs roughly USD 200–400 per month.

## Transport

- **Tuk-tuk / ride-hailing (e.g. Loca)**: about USD 1–3 for short trips.
- **Motorbike rental**: about USD 60–120 per month.
- **Buying a vehicle**: plenty of used options, but import duties are high.

## Telecom

- **Local SIM**: Unitel, Lao Telecom, ETL and others; data plans about USD 5–15 per month.
- Coverage along the China–Laos Railway has improved significantly.

## Healthcare and insurance

Public healthcare is limited; foreigners often use international clinics or travel to Thailand. **International health insurance is strongly recommended** — a single emergency can be very expensive.

## Monthly budget reference (single person)

| Lifestyle | Estimated monthly cost |
| --- | --- |
| Frugal (shared room, local food, motorbike) | USD 500–800 |
| Comfortable (own apartment, mixed food, some taxis) | USD 900–1500 |
| Premium (downtown apartment, eating out, car) | USD 1500+ |

## Money and payments

- The official currency is the **Lao kip (LAK)**; US dollars and Thai baht are accepted by some businesses.
- Cash is still common; mobile payments (e.g. OnePay) are growing in cities.
- Exchange at banks or licensed counters rather than the street.

> These are general estimates. Actual costs vary and are affected by exchange rates and inflation.
